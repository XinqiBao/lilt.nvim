# Two fables

Fables traditionally attributed to Aesop (ancient Greece), in George Fyler
Townsend's (1814–1900) English translation, *Three Hundred Æsop's Fables*.

Text sources are these Wikisource transcriptions of George Routledge and Sons
editions, checked when preparing the sample:

- [The Fox and the Grapes, p. 128](https://en.wikisource.org/w/index.php?title=Three_Hundred_Æsop%27s_Fables/The_Fox_and_the_Grapes&oldid=11816478)
  (the source page identifies the edition as 1887).
- [The North Wind and the Sun, pp. 154–155](https://en.wikisource.org/w/index.php?title=Three_Hundred_Æsop%27s_Fables/The_North_Wind_and_the_Sun&oldid=11817796)
  (the source page identifies the edition as 1867).

Headings are omitted, initial small capitals become ordinary capitalization,
and prose is wrapped into physical lines of at most 72 columns. Words,
punctuation, and the separate moral paragraph are retained. The page break
within the second fable is joined; it is not a paragraph break.

The ancient fables, Townsend's translation, and these nineteenth-century
editions are in the public domain in the United States and in countries with
a life-plus-70-year copyright term. No modern translation, illustrations, or
editorial material is included. The plugin's MIT code license does not
relicense the literary text.
